1.1 Introduction
The flood is a natural phenomenon that emerges rather quickly and may cause very serious damages to people, roads, settlements and even to infrastructure and services that work without interruptions. Floods depend on many factors: the intensity and amount of rainfall, duration of their presence, atmospheric conditions and the natural relief and geographical position of the area. Effective management of the natural disaster depends not only on its prediction but also on the analysis and transformation of the environmental information into the risk information.
Despite the fact that the traditional weather and warning systems provide certain useful information, the risk of the flooding for any particular location cannot be estimated using separate parameters. It is possible to solve the problem of flooding effectively with the help of AI, by using the information related to the historical and modern climate and environment, as well as other pieces of information in order to engineer valuable features and engineered environmental features that will be later analyzed using the core of SANKET - SOALM (Self Operated AI Learning Model). First, data acquisition and pre-processing layer that extracts relevant data from the historical climate and environmental sensors. Second, with the help of the core of SOALM AI risk intelligence layer, it is possible to use the engineered features in the previous layer and analyze them using the core of SANKET, SOALM.
1.1.1 Motivation
The major motivation of SANKET creation is in bridging the gap between the weather monitoring and the assessment of the flood risk. The weather monitoring service provides the following information: temperature, pressure, humidity, wind speed and rainfall in the form of current values and forecasts. One cannot evaluate the risk level based on this information. SANKET makes use of artificial intelligence technology to analyze various weather patterns that increase the risk of flooding.
Apart from the mentioned above characteristics of the weather data, the historical data about the rainfall is much more significant than the current rainfall for assessing the environmental vulnerability. Even the small amount of the rainfall during a long period of time may cause the environmental damage. Thus, in SANKET, the data of rainfall is analyzed for different durations of precipitation.
The SANKET Project will also try to make the predictions generated by it Spatially Understandable, i.e., a prediction for a particular location will be accompanied by predictions for other locations in the vicinity of the former one. This way, the users will be able to generate GIS-based risk maps that will allow them to concentrate on areas of higher risk.

1.1.2 Problem Objective
The primary goals of the SANKET are:
1.1.2.1 Development of Flood-Risk Intelligence System using historical and real-time environmental data based on AI.
1.1.2.2 Engineering of multi-temporal weather features, particularly, rainfalls' accumulation for different time windows.
1.1.2.3 Classification and prediction of the flood conditions and subsequent categorization of the latter in understandable risk classes with probabilities.
1.1.2.4 Generation of spatial risk predictions rather than scores of predictions for particular points.
1.1.2.5 Ability to visualize the predicted risk in GIS environment and connect it with appropriate emergency resources.
1.1.2.6 Development of a framework for integration of future data, such as terrain, hydrological, satellite, IoT and What-If simulations.
1.1.3 Scope of the Project
SANKET is a project for the development of integrated AI-based flood-risk intelligence framework based on historical climate and meteorological information to assess risks via discovery of temporal, atmospheric and geographical patterns.
The considered framework within SANKET scope includes accumulation of rainfalls for different time horizons, trends in atmospheric parameters, influence of seasonality and geospatial attributes. Then, SANKET-integrated intelligence SOALM uses this information to classify a situation into different risk-levels with probabilities.
It can also generate risk predictions for neighboring locations and provide information on this risk in a spatial manner, overlaying it with information on shelters, hospitals, police stations and fire stations, etc.
The basic framework for the risk-aware routing and the What-If analysis of the environmental conditions like rainfalls is already created. SANKET will be further enriched with Digital Elevation Models, water level and flow measurement of rivers, satellite images as well as with IoT sensors' data and other data related to disaster.

1.1.4 Historical Climate Data and Feature Engineering
Most importantly, SANKET should get trained deeply on historical climate and other meteorological data to make the SANKET's flood risk intelligence system "Seasoned and Experienced". The historical climate and other meteorological data should pass quality check, synchronizing of time stamps, handling of missing values, etc. before feature engineering for modeling.
One of the characteristics of SANKET is the accumulation of rainfall over multiple time horizons. Rather than considering only the current weather data, accumulation of rainfall over 1 hour, 3 hours, 6 hours, 12 hours, 24 hours, 48 hours and 72 hours is considered in order to differentiate between the short rainfalls and prolonged precipitation. 
Also, along with the accumulation of rainfall, the temperature, humidity, dew point, atmospheric pressure, wind speed and wind direction, total cloud cover, time of the year and also geographical factors such as latitude and longitude of the location are considered. Also, the past trends and variations in these factors can be included.
Pipeline for Feature Engineering from Historical Climate Data
Climate data are initially preprocessed and transformed into temporal, meteorological and geographical features, which are used as an input to the SOALM intelligence layer.
1.1.5 SOALM-Based Flood Risk Intelligence
Self Operated AI Learning Model (SOALM) – Intelligence Layer: This is the core component of the SANKET application. It is a machine learning algorithm that can use the multiple environmental inputs and give the output about the risk of flood in four different levels namely LOW, MODERATE, HIGH and CRITICAL. This model can give the class probability that will help us to understand the level of risk in the uncertain environment. In addition to that, this model can perform the spatial predictions for the multiple locations at once and also gives a grid of predicted values about the risk of flood at the target location.
These probability values can then be used in order to assess the level of risk for rapidly changing or uncertain environmental conditions. For instance, short duration rainfalls can create rapidly changing risk levels within minutes or hours.
Spatial prediction can be done by the SOALM algorithm for multiple coordinates in the vicinity of the target location in order to create a grid of predictions for the risk of flood at the target location. After that, the risk information can be combined with the GIS data to create the risk zone map that helps the user to know about the distribution and concentration of the risk information in the space.
SOALM Flood-Risk Intelligence and Spatial Prediction Framework
SOALM translates the engineered environmental features into risk classifications and generates a grid of predictions for the surrounding region(s).
1.1.6 GIS-Based Disaster Decision Support
For enabling effective use of the forecast data for planning of the emergency response activities, SANKET provides a GIS-based visualization of the flooded areas and of the geographical and emergency information around the predicted hazard.
The spatial risk map provided by SANKET can then be merged with nearby hospitals, shelters, police stations, fire stations, and other important infrastructures. This will help the user to know the hazard and the nearby resources to manage the emergency situation.
This spatial information will be used for hazard-aware evacuation routing, i.e., routes will be selected based not only on the shortest path but also on the expected flood-risk level. It is also possible to conduct the “what-if” scenario analysis in a 3D/terrain-based manner, i.e., the users can conduct and analyze the impact of the changes in rainfall or other environmental parameters on the expected flood risk at the various locations.
GIS-Based Disaster Decision-Support Architecture
SOALM Spatial Predictions of Flooding overlaid on Geographical and Emergency Resources to support Preparation, Response and “What-If” Scenario Planning.

1.2 Previous Work-
1.2.1 Bentivoglio et al. ( 2022)
Bentivoglio et al. (2022) review several deep-learning approaches for flood mapping. In particular, they highlight the importance of the combination of a relatively simple prediction task with the information regarding the spatial position of the area affected by the flood. They also mention several limitations related to the regional generalization problem and the uncertainty related to the flood mapping. The works by Bentivoglio et al. (2022) provide the following support to SANKET: (1) spatial risk visualization related to the four-level flood-risk classification; (2) grid-based inference, which is much simpler than those reviewed by the authors and more appropriate for practical implementation and validation.
1.2.2 Kordopatis et al. ( 2023)
There have been similar studies for flood monitoring and forecasting using machine learning. Such an approach has been proposed by Kordopatis et al. (2023) and validated using an experimental setup. The approach includes several layers, namely data, intelligence, and spatial visualization. While the approach used in the work by Kordopatis et al. (2023) is based on long-term historical learning, the SANKET approach relies on modular development.
1.2.3 Zhang et al. ( 2024)
This work considered cross-region flood/streamflow forecasting models based on spatial information. Spatial information and grids were also used in SANKET, but the applications were limited to four-level flood-risk classification.
1.2.4 Yi and Yi (2024)
Yi and Yi (2024) presented the comparative study of machine learning and deep learning approaches for flood forecasting and demonstrated that the high complexity does not necessarily mean the better results, and the algorithm choice depends highly on the data and application. Therefore, in the case of proposed SANKET system, the comparative study should be performed before the fixation of the parameters of the selected Simple Organism-motivated ALgorithmic Μάchtiny Layer, SOALM.
1.2.5 Asif et al. ( 2025)
Asif et al. (2025) evaluated applications of machine-learning techniques for the short-term flood forecasting and stated that accuracy was not the best criteria for the performance of the models for this kind of problem. Thus, in addition to accuracy, SANKET will also utilize precision, recall, F1-score, class-wise performance, and other probability-related measures to assess the performance of its SOALM. Besides, the data from SANKET will be used to make decisions for the spatially distributed decisions.

1.2.7 Research Gap
The literature reveals four main gaps relevant to SANKET:
•	Long-term historical learning with well-prepared and validated data.
•	Explicit uncertainty communication via probability and confidence information.
•	Spatial risk assessment beyond point-based predictions.
•	Decision-oriented integration via GIS and emergency information.
These four aspects were addressed in SANKET along with recommendations for further validation and implementation.

1.2.8 Research Gaps and System Requirements
The first research gap concerns the data completeness for historical learning using a validated data set. Although a fair number of previous approaches used short-term data sets from one or several events, or several years of data, SANKET uses a huge data set consisting of about 5 million records and covering a fifty-to seventy-year period (1940-2026/27).
The second research gap addressed by SANKET is concerned with the issue of communication. As opposed to presenting only a numeric score, SANKET provides 4 risk levels that can be used in conjunction with the probability of such risk level occurring in order to better comprehend the uncertainty.
Thirdly, there is the issue of spatial context. Most models generate single point predictions. However, there may be considerable variability in the neighboring area as well. Spatial risk pattern generation is accomplished using a grid-based approach in SANKET.

1.4 Proposed Feasibility
Technically, the feasibility of this project is high as it is very modular. It is possible to begin with data set preparation and then continue working on the baseline model, feature engineering, evaluation, API integration, and finally GIS visualization.
Although the data set is large, and hence storage and processing will be challenging, the model can still be tested on sampled data for the purposes of experiments. In addition, the modularity of the components makes it easy to improve individual model components without needing to re-engineer the whole system.

1.4.1 Proposed Methodology
1.4.1.1 Workflows Overview
The proposed methodology will proceed according to the following pipeline:
1. 1. Data collection: 1940-2026 climate historical data collection and input data preparation for experiments.
2. 2. Data cleaning: missing, duplicate, inconsistent and invalid records check.
3. 3. Six-hour alignment: align all observations to six-hour timeline.
4. 4. Feature engineering: creating about 40 features of rainfall, atmospheric, time and spatial nature.
5. 5. Dataset splitting: utilizing time-aware validation for avoiding temporal leakage.
6. 6. Model training: training and comparison of different machine-learning models.
7. 7. Model evaluation: measuring model's performance per class, confusion matrix of the model and output probability.
8. 8. SOALM inference: calculation of risk probabilities and the risk class.
9. 9. Spatial prediction: making prediction in the vicinity of input cells.
10. 10. GIS visualization: visualizing results as spatial risk zones.
1.4.2 Human View on the Workflow
SANKET – The Time Expert: The name SANKET, as a nickname of the SANKET system is based on two key words. First, the word "time" refers to the fact that the SANKET system works with the weather conditions throughout time and not only with one observation. The input for SANKET is a set of data concerning recent rain, its duration, other changes in the atmosphere and season peculiarities. All this data is transformed into a set of features of the model. SOALM model makes the probabilities of risks at the input location and spread them to neighboring cells to get spatial risk maps.
1.4.3 SOALM Prediction Algorithm
Let the feature vector be x = [x₁, x₂, …, xₙ]. SOALM calculates the probability of each risk class and selects the class with the highest probability:
ŷ = argmax P(C = k | x)
where k stands for LOW, MODERATE, HIGH or CRITICAL.
Although we can use the highest class probability as a measure of confidence, the probability calibration (e.g., plotting a reliability curve) still should be studied, as model's confidence does not necessarily mean reliability.

1.4.5 Spatiotemporal Prediction Approach
Given the spatial grid G={g₁, g₂, …, gₘ}, SANKET computes the predictors for each cell and produces the prediction for the cell. The predictions are saved along with their coordinates and geometry, presented as a GIS risk zone.
Grid density can still be set, as bigger grids have higher resolution, though they demand more predictions to be produced and processed.
1.4.6 Model Selection Criterion
While selecting the final model, we take into account not just accuracy, but class-wise accuracy, stability of validation results, inference speed, and prediction quality. In case of close models, we select the simpler one that can be easily deployed.
